\paragraph{Individual pairs against their own clean rerun.}
We also compare each perturbed pair of $\pi_0$ under Sensor Noise (primary scope) with the clean-rerun distance of its own setting. After control 5, let $d_{vn}$ be the end-effector position distance between $T_n$ and $R_n$ for setting $v$ and noise draw $n$, and let $r_v$ be the distance between that setting's $R_0$ and $R_1$. Each recorded pair is classed by $d_{vn}<r_v$, $d_{vn}=r_v$ or $d_{vn}>r_v$, and all four outcome groups are kept (Table~\ref{tab:sensor-case-reference}).

Most pairs that lose a success moved less than a clean rerun: of the 18 lost-success pairs, 12 have $d_{vn}<r_v$. In every setting with a lost-success pair, both clean runs succeed, so the clean rerun changes no outcome there. The twelve pairs cover ten settings and eight tasks; all lost-success pairs cover fifteen settings and ten tasks. All 55 settings on 28 tasks have both perturbed pairs, measured distances after control 5, a positive clean-rerun distance and known outcomes. The two pairs of a setting share one rerun distance, so the counts are nested within settings: they are a descriptive split, while the task-weighted estimates above keep the hierarchy.

\paragraph{Motion size is a poor predictor of lost success (post hoc AUROC).}
The main paper summarizes these pairs by an AUROC, computed post hoc: the probability that a lost-success pair has a larger ratio $d_{vn}/r_v$ than a pair that keeps its success (ties count one half; one half is chance, one is perfect). The 18 lost-success pairs are the positive class and the 84 joint-success pairs the negative class, pooled without weights. The AUROC is $0.49$, close to chance, with 95\% interval $[0.29,0.70]$: the percentile interval of 2{,}000 whole-task bootstrap draws that resample the 28 tasks with replacement and keep each task's pairs together, so the two pairs of a setting keep their shared $r_v$. Every draw contained both classes. With families pooled, the raw distance gives $0.73$ ($\pi_{0.5}$) and $0.64$ ($\pi_0$), and the norm of the change in the first five commands at the first query gives $0.74$ and $0.64$. Pooled values can reflect differences between families; comparing pairs only within their family gives $0.62\ [0.48,0.74]$ and $0.55\ [0.35,0.70]$ for the ratio. Per-family values: Sec.~\ref{sec:five-arm}.
