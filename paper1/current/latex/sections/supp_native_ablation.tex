\section{Removing the Shared L9 Directions without a Clean Observation}
\label{sec:supp-native-ablation}

Restoration (Sec.~\ref{sec:allstep-restoration}) needs a clean observation of the same state. This test needs none: instead of copying clean values back at every generation step, it removes part of the L9 state's component along the shared directions, measured from a clean training mean, only at the first generation step of each query. In $\pi_{0.5}$ on original LIBERO (6{,}048 planned rollouts), the edit lowers the response at L18 by $8.8\%$ under a camera change and $7.6\%$ under headlight removal. Clean-feature directions with the same mean edit size raise it ($-2.5/-3.6\%$ reduction), and random directions lower it by $0.1$--$0.3\%$. It changes the first five commands by a small fraction of the perturbation's change and does not measurably change task success against the unedited policy. This supports the second test in main Sec.~\ref*{M-sec:restoration}.

\paragraph{Design.}
The edit $H'=H-\lambda[(H-\mu_H)B]B^\top$ removes a fraction $\lambda$ of the action-expert L9 state's deviation from the clean training mean $\mu_H$ along the 16 directions $B$, at all ten plan positions and only at the first generation step of each policy query; later steps and all other layers run unchanged. The directions are the L9 shared directions of the cross-observation study (Sec.~\ref{sec:supp-p08-dynamic}), not the LIBERO-Plus fits. The controls use clean-feature or random directions with the same training-set mean edit norm over the three conditions, so that they differ in direction, not amount; this is Shared-large, and Shared-small uses half that dose. The design has 16 held-out tasks $\times$ 6 start states $\times$ 3 policy-noise draws $=$ 288 cases; each case runs 3 visual conditions (clean, Camera $+15^\circ$, headlight off) $\times$ 7 configurations (Unedited, Shared-small, Shared-large, Clean-feature, Random-1/2/3) $=$ 6{,}048 planned main episodes, plus 16 technical repeats. Table~\ref{tab:supp-native-definitions} defines each quantity. Matching within each condition did not meet the 2\% tolerance, so the actual edit $L_2$ per condition is reported with the comparison.

\paragraph{Only the shared-direction edits lower the L18 response.}
The endpoint is the per-case reduction of the L18 response energy, $1-\|\Delta_{\mathrm{edited}}\|^2/\|\Delta_{\mathrm{unedited}}\|^2$, averaged with equal task weights; positive values mean a smaller response. The half-dose edit (Shared-small) lowers it by $4.2/3.6\%$ (Table~\ref{tab:supp-native-ablation}).

\paragraph{The command change is small relative to the perturbation's.}
The edit's footprint, the edited-minus-unedited RMS of the first five commands (translation part) on the same input, is $0.0006$ (Camera) and $0.0006$ (headlight), about $0.5\%$ and $1.2\%$ of the change the perturbation itself causes (unedited perturbed-minus-clean RMS $0.128$ and $0.051$). Rotation and the continuous gripper value are separate endpoints. In the 120-case interim analysis the formatted gripper output was identical in all 3{,}840 non-self pairs although the continuous gripper value differed in 3{,}837 (an interim finding, not recomputed on the full population). This edit acts only at the first generation step; restoration, which acts at every step, moves the commands far more (Sec.~\ref{sec:dev-restoration-controls}).

\paragraph{The edit does not measurably change task success.}
Success is reported as bounds over missing outcomes, because the prespecified rule withholds point estimates and ordinary intervals whenever a planned outcome is missing (Table~\ref{tab:supp-native-ablation}). Within each condition, the known lost and gained successes from unedited to Shared-large are $2.4/1.4$, $2.4/3.1$ and $1.4/3.1\%$ of planned pairs (clean/Camera/headlight; unknown pairs $1.7$--$2.1\%$). Table~\ref{tab:supp-native-contrasts} gives the nine prespecified contrasts: Shared-large minus Unedited ($D$), minus Clean-feature ($Q$), or minus the mean of the three random sets ($R$), under each visual condition.

Over the 111 missing planned outcomes, only Camera $Q$ keeps its sign under every assignment: it is positive, favoring the shared directions over the clean-feature directions. The other eight bounds include zero. The Clean-feature edit's own bounds exclude zero in each condition (Table~\ref{tab:supp-native-ablation}): it lowers success under clean input and Camera and raises it under headlight removal. For the unedited policy, Camera's success bounds are $91.0$--$93.1\%$, against $92.4$--$93.4\%$ under clean input, whereas headlight removal costs $2.8$--$5.9$ points. 

\paragraph{Post hoc sampling intervals on the known outcomes.}
A post hoc bootstrap of the 5,937 known outcomes over the 16 held-out tasks (2,000 draws, task-equal means inside each draw) assesses sampling variation. Table~\ref{tab:supp-native-contrasts} reports its point estimates, pointwise 95\% intervals and Bonferroni-adjusted intervals at $1-0.05/9$ beside the bounds. Camera $Q$ and $R$ have positive pointwise and adjusted intervals; the other seven intervals include zero. For Camera $Q$, the adjusted interval $[+0.3,+13.2]$ and the bounds $[+2.8,+6.6]$ both favor the shared directions over the clean-feature directions. For Camera $R$, the adjusted interval is positive while the bounds include zero, so the comparison with random directions depends on the analysis of known outcomes. Shared-large against Unedited includes zero in all three conditions.

\paragraph{Missing outcomes.}
Acquisition resumed in the prespecified order after a pause; interim results had been observed, but case selection did not depend on outcomes. 281 cases completed all 21 runs, 6 completed partially (90 missing runs), and one was interrupted after creation; none remained unexecuted. In total, 5,937 of 6{,}048 main outcomes are known. All planned outcomes remain in the estimand.

\paragraph{Development check of the schedule.}
With edits only at the first generation step, the L18 difference at the last step retains 3.7--5.5\% of the first-step difference (12 development inputs); editing every generation step changes the commands about ten times more, with a reduction of about 2\% in the visual difference (Spatial task 7, Camera).
